# Prohibited Technologies & Covered Applications

Juno is a state-owned system operated by UT Dallas. Under Texas law (Governor Abbott's executive orders and **Senate Bill 1893**, 88th Texas Legislature), certain foreign-owned applications and hardware are prohibited on state-owned devices and networks — including Juno's login and compute nodes.

As a Juno user, **you must not install, run, or store prohibited technologies anywhere on the cluster** (home, work, scratch, or group directories). Doing so violates state policy and UT Dallas rules and may result in removal of the software and loss of access.

## Authoritative source

The official lists are maintained by the Texas Department of Information Resources (DIR), change frequently, and are the definitive reference. **Always confirm here before installing unfamiliar software** — do not treat this page as the primary source:

- **[DIR — Covered Applications & Prohibited Technologies](https://dir.texas.gov/information-security/covered-applications-and-prohibited-technologies)**

## What matters most for HPC

Several prohibited developers distribute AI/ML models and tooling that users may be tempted to download for research. **Do not download, install, or run models or software from these on Juno** (this list is not exhaustive — check the DIR source):

- **DeepSeek**, **Baichuan**, **Moonshot AI**, **MiniMax**, **StepFun**, **iFlytek**

The broader DIR lists also cover consumer apps (TikTok, WeChat, Lemon8, RedNote, Temu, CamScanner, WPS Office, Kaspersky, …) and hardware manufacturers (Huawei, DJI, Hikvision, TP-Link, ZTE, Xiaomi, …). Do not use prohibited hardware to connect to Juno.
